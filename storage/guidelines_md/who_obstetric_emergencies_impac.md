# WHO Integrated Management of Pregnancy & Childbirth (PCPNC / IMPAC): Obstetric Emergencies & Danger Signs
Source: World Health Organization (PCPNC Guide for Essential Practice, 3rd Edition)
Clinical Scope: Triage Algorithms, Major Obstetric Danger Signs, and Emergency Management

---

## 1. Cardinal Obstetric Danger Signs (RED - Immediate Emergency)
Any pregnant woman with one or more of the following symptoms must be classified as **RED (Immediate Obstetric Emergency)** and referred immediately to a Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC) facility or General Hospital:

### 1.1 Vaginal Bleeding (Haemorrhage)
- **First Trimester (<14 weeks):**
  - Causes: Threatened, inevitable, incomplete miscarriage, or ruptured ectopic pregnancy.
  - *Ectopic Pregnancy Red Flags:* Unilateral severe pelvic or lower abdominal pain, shoulder tip pain (diaphragmatic irritation from hemoperitoneum), dizziness, fainting, tachycardia, hypotension.
- **Second and Third Trimesters (>20 weeks - Antepartum Haemorrhage):**
  - **Placenta Praevia:** Painless, recurrent, bright red vaginal bleeding. Uterus is soft and non-tender. *Crucial Rule:* NEVER perform a digital vaginal examination (PV exam) outside an operating theatre with setup for immediate Caesarean section!
  - **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae):** Painful, dark red vaginal bleeding (may be concealed), continuous severe abdominal/back pain, wooden hard ("board-like") tense uterus, fetal distress or absent heart tones, maternal shock.

### 1.2 Severe Pre-Eclampsia and Eclampsia
- **Definitions:**
  - *Gestational Hypertension:* Systolic BP >= 140 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP >= 90 mmHg arising after 20 weeks of gestation.
  - *Severe Pre-Eclampsia:* BP >= 160/110 mmHg with proteinuria, accompanied by impending eclampsia symptoms:
    - Severe, persistent, throbbing frontal or occipital headache unresponsive to paracetamol.
    - Visual disturbances: Blurring of vision, scotomata (flashing spots), double vision, temporary blindness.
    - Severe epigastric or right upper quadrant pain (hepatic swelling / Glisson's capsule distension).
    - Hyperreflexia or brisk deep tendon reflexes.
    - Oliguria (<400 ml in 24 hours).
  - *Eclampsia:* Occurrence of generalized tonic-clonic convulsions in a woman with pre-eclampsia not attributable to other cerebral causes.
- **Immediate Life-Saving Management:**
  - Drug of Choice: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4)** for convulsion control and prevention.
  - Protect airway, turn patient on left lateral position, administer oxygen, control severe hypertension with intravenous labetalol or oral hydralazine/nifedipine. Immediate delivery is the definitive cure.

### 1.3 Maternal Sepsis and Chorioamnionitis
- **Symptoms:** High fever (temperature >= 38°C / 100.4°F), foul-smelling or purulent vaginal discharge, severe lower abdominal tenderness, maternal tachycardia (>100 bpm), fetal tachycardia (>160 bpm).
- **Action:** Urgent hospital admission, IV broad-spectrum antibiotics, fluid resuscitation, and planned delivery.

### 1.4 Preterm Premature Rupture of Membranes (PPROM) and Cord Prolapse
- **Sudden gush or continuous leaking of watery fluid from the vagina** before 37 weeks.
- **Cord Prolapse:** If umbilical cord is visible or palpable in the vagina, this is an ultra-emergency. Position woman in knee-chest or steep Trendelenburg position, elevate presenting fetal part manually with a sterile gloved hand, and rush for emergency Caesarean section.

### 1.5 Absent or Markedly Reduced Fetal Movements
- Fetal kick counts: A healthy fetus moves at least 10 times over a 2-hour period during maternal rest.
- Significant reduction or total absence of fetal movement for more than 12 hours in the 3rd trimester warrants urgent cardiotocography (CTG) or obstetric ultrasound to evaluate fetal viability.

## 2. Priority / Urgent Obstetric Conditions (YELLOW - Urgent Same-Day Review)
Conditions that require medical evaluation within 12 to 24 hours to prevent escalation to emergencies:
- Persistent fever without severe sepsis signs (Suspected Malaria, Typhoid, or Urinary Tract Infection).
- Severe burning sensation during urination (Dysuria) or flank pain (Pyelonephritis triggers premature labor).
- Severe intractable vomiting (Hyperemesis Gravidarum) unable to retain oral fluids for >24 hours.
- Severe bilateral pitting edema of feet and legs without hypertension or headache.
- Moderate generalized itching especially on palms and soles in the third trimester (Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy).
