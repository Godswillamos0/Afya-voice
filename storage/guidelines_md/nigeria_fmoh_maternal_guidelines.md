# Federal Ministry of Health (FMOH) Nigeria: Maternal and Antenatal Care Guidelines
Source: Federal Ministry of Health Nigeria (National Guidelines for Basic Newborn and Maternal Care)
Jurisdiction: Federal Republic of Nigeria (Primary Health Centers, General Hospitals, and Federal Medical Centres)

---

## 1. Focused Antenatal Care (FANC) Package in Nigeria
The Federal Ministry of Health of Nigeria mandates that all pregnant women access comprehensive, respectful, and culturally congruent maternal care:
- Universal registration and early booking before 14 weeks of gestation.
- Maternal profiling: Blood group, Rhesus factor, Hemoglobin electrophoresis (Genotype screening for Sickle Cell Disease - HbSS/SC/AA/AS), rapid syphilis testing (VDRL/RPR), HIV testing and counseling (PMTCT protocol), and Hepatitis B/C screening.
- Routine clinical monitoring at every visit: Blood pressure, maternal weight, fundal height, fetal heart rate, urine dipstick for protein and glucose, and conjunctival/mucosal inspection for anemia.

---

## 2. Malaria in Pregnancy (MiP) Protocol for Nigeria
Malaria is the leading cause of maternal anemia, spontaneous abortion, low birth weight, and stillbirth in Nigeria.
### 2.1 Intermittent Preventive Treatment (IPTp-SP)
- **Eligibility:** All pregnant women living in Nigeria, regardless of symptoms.
- **Drug:** Sulfadoxine 500 mg + Pyrimethamine 25 mg (SP / Fansidar).
- **Dosage:** 3 tablets oral single dose.
- **Timing:** Initiate at the beginning of the second trimester (after quickening or 13 weeks). Repeat at every scheduled ANC visit at least 4 weeks apart, up to delivery (minimum 3 to 5 doses).
- **Contraindications:** Do not administer SP in the first trimester. Do not administer to women taking daily Co-trimoxazole prophylaxis (e.g. HIV-positive patients).
- **Bed Nets:** Universal provision and daily use of Long-Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLINs).

### 2.2 Treatment of Confirmed Malaria in Pregnancy in Nigeria
- **First Trimester:** Oral Quinine + Clindamycin for 7 days (or Artemether-Lumefantrine if quinine unavailable).
- **Second and Third Trimesters:** Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT) - Artemether-Lumefantrine (AL) or Artesunate-Amodiaquine.
- **Severe Malaria in Pregnancy:** Parenteral Artesunate (IV or IM) is the drug of choice in all trimesters, followed by a full course of oral ACT once the patient can take oral medication.

## 3. Anemia Prevention and Nutritional Supplementation
- In Nigeria, over 55% of pregnant women experience iron deficiency anemia.
- **Routine Prophylaxis:**
  - Ferrous Sulfate 200 mg (equivalent to 60 mg elemental iron) daily.
  - Folic Acid 400 µg to 5 mg daily throughout pregnancy.
- **Dietary Counseling with Indigenous Nigerian Foods:**
  - Encourage consumption of iron-rich and folate-rich indigenous foods: Ugwu leaves (fluted pumpkin), Bitter leaf, Scent leaf, Spinach, Beans (cowpeas), Soya beans, Eggs, Fish, Liver, and fresh citrus fruits (Oranges, Pawpaw) rich in Vitamin C.

---

## 4. Tetanus Toxoid Immunization (TT / Td Schedule)
- **TT1 / Td1:** At first contact or booking.
- **TT2 / Td2:** At least 4 weeks after TT1 (gives 3 years protection; must be given at least 2 weeks before expected delivery).
- **TT3 / Td3:** At least 6 months after TT2 (gives 5 years protection).
- **TT4 / Td4:** At least 1 year after TT3 (gives 10 years protection).
- **TT5 / Td5:** At least 1 year after TT4 (gives lifetime protection).

---

## 5. Birth Preparedness and Complication Readiness (BPCR) Plan
Every pregnant woman and her family in Nigeria must formulate an individualized BPCR plan by the 24th week:
1. **Accredited Facility:** Identify the nearest Primary Health Center (PHC) or General Hospital with 24/7 skilled birth attendants (doctor/midwife).
2. **Transportation Arrangement:** Identify reliable commercial or community emergency transport available 24/7.
3. **Emergency Funds:** Save dedicated emergency funds for medical commodities, medications, and hospital admission.
4. **Emergency Blood Donor:** Identify two family members or community members with compatible blood group willing to donate in case of postpartum hemorrhage.
5. **Essential Birth Kit (Mama Kit):** Clean delivery mat, sterile razor blade/cord clamp, chlorhexidine 7.1% gel for newborn umbilical cord care, clean cotton wool, sanitary pads, soap, and clean baby clothing.
6. **Emergency Hotlines:** Know the national emergency line (**112**) and local facility telephone contacts.
