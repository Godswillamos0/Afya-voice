# WHO Recommendations: Intrapartum Care & Maternal Postnatal Experience (2018 & 2022)
Source: World Health Organization (ISBN: 978 92 4 155021 5 & ISBN: 978 92 4 004598 9)
Clinical Scope: Labor, Childbirth, Postpartum Care, and Danger Signs

---

## 1. Intrapartum Care (Childbirth & Labor)
### 1.1 True Labor vs. False Labor (Braxton Hicks)
- **False Labor (Braxton Hicks):** Irregular uterine contractions, variable intensity, discomfort primarily in lower abdomen and groin, contractions do not increase in frequency or intensity over time, cervix does not dilate, pain subsides with rest, walking, or warm bath.
- **True Labor:** Regular, rhythmic uterine contractions occurring every 3 to 5 minutes, progressively becoming longer, stronger, and more frequent. Radiates from lower back around to the lower abdomen. Associated with cervical dilation, effacement, and often "bloody show" (mucus plug mixed with blood).

### 1.2 Respectful Maternity Care & Labor Support
- A companion of choice (partner, family member, doula) is recommended for every woman throughout labor and childbirth.
- Freedom in labor: Women should be encouraged to move around and adopt their position of choice during labor and childbirth (walking, upright, squatting, or side-lying; avoid prolonged supine position).
- Adequate oral hydration and light nutritious snacks are recommended during active labor for low-risk women.

### 1.3 Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL) & PPH Prevention
- Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH) is the single largest killer of mothers worldwide.
- **Prophylactic Uterotonics:** The administration of **Oxytocin 10 IU (IM or IV)** immediately after the birth of the baby is universally recommended for all births to prevent PPH.
- Delayed umbilical cord clamping (not earlier than 1 to 3 minutes after birth) is recommended for all neonates to improve infant iron stores.

## 2. Maternal Postnatal Care (The First 6 Weeks)
The postnatal period (from immediately after birth up to 6 weeks) carries the highest risk of maternal mortality.

### 2.1 Postpartum Maternal Danger Signs (RED - Immediate Hospital Emergency)
A woman in the postnatal period must be rushed to hospital immediately if she presents with:
1. **Severe Postpartum Haemorrhage:** Heavy vaginal bleeding soaking more than two large sanitary pads per hour for two consecutive hours, or passing large blood clots (size of an orange).
2. **Puerperal Sepsis:** Fever (temperature >= 38°C), chills, and foul-smelling or purulent vaginal discharge (lochia), accompanied by lower abdominal pain.
3. **Severe Postpartum Pre-Eclampsia:** Severe throbbing headache, blurred or double vision, flashing lights, severe upper belly pain, and marked hypertension occurring within 6 weeks of delivery.
4. **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) / Pulmonary Embolism:** Unilateral severe calf pain, tenderness, redness, and swelling, or sudden chest pain and shortness of breath.
5. **Postpartum Depression and Psychosis:** Severe insomnia, extreme exhaustion, detachment from or urge to harm the newborn, severe anxiety, auditory hallucinations, or feelings of despair. Immediate mental healthcare referral is essential.

### 2.2 Routine Postnatal Care Schedule
WHO recommends a minimum of four postnatal care contacts for healthy mothers and newborns:
- **Contact 1:** Within 24 hours of birth (at health facility before discharge).
- **Contact 2:** Between 48 and 72 hours after birth.
- **Contact 3:** Between days 7 and 14 after birth.
- **Contact 4:** At 6 weeks after birth (includes postpartum family planning counseling, screening for anemia, and baby's 6-week immunization: Pentavalent 1, PCV 1, Rota 1, OPV 1).

### 2.3 Exclusive Breastfeeding Guidance
- Early initiation of exclusive breastfeeding within the first hour of birth.
- Colostrum ("first milk", yellowish and thick) is rich in maternal antibodies and must NEVER be discarded.
- No prelacteal feeds (no plain water, glucose water, artificial formula, or herbal teas) during the first 6 months of life.
