# WHO Recommendations on Antenatal Care for a Positive Pregnancy Experience (2016)
Source: World Health Organization (ISBN: 978 92 4 154991 2)
Guideline Authority: WHO Department of Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health

---

## 1. ANC Model & Schedule (The 8-Contact Model)
The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends a minimum of **eight (8) antenatal care contacts** to reduce perinatal mortality and improve maternal health outcomes:
- **Contact 1:** Up to 12 weeks of pregnancy (First trimester)
- **Contact 2:** 20 weeks of pregnancy
- **Contact 3:** 26 weeks of pregnancy
- **Contact 4:** 30 weeks of pregnancy
- **Contact 5:** 34 weeks of pregnancy
- **Contact 6:** 36 weeks of pregnancy
- **Contact 7:** 38 weeks of pregnancy
- **Contact 8:** 40 weeks of pregnancy (or at delivery)

## 2. Maternal Nutritional Interventions
### Routine Daily Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation
- **Recommendation:** Daily oral iron and folic acid supplementation with 30 mg to 60 mg of elemental iron and 400 µg (0.4 mg) of folic acid is recommended for pregnant women to prevent maternal anaemia, puerperal sepsis, low birth weight, and preterm birth.
- In settings where anaemia in pregnant women is a severe public health problem (prevalence of 40% or higher, such as Nigeria and West Africa), a daily dose of **60 mg of elemental iron** is preferred.
- **Counseling Tip:** Take with clean water or citrus fruit juice (Vitamin C enhances absorption). Do not take with tea, coffee, or milk. Constipation or dark stools are harmless side effects.

### Calcium Supplementation in High-Risk Populations
- **Recommendation:** In populations with low dietary calcium intake, daily calcium supplementation (1.5 g to 2.0 g oral elemental calcium per day, divided into three doses taken with meals) is recommended for pregnant women to reduce the risk of pre-eclampsia.

## 3. Management of Common Physiological Pregnancy Symptoms
### Nausea and Vomiting (Morning Sickness)
- **Clinical Evaluation:** Common in the first trimester (up to 14 weeks). Differentiate physiological morning sickness from **Hyperemesis Gravidarum** (severe intractable vomiting, weight loss >5%, dehydration, and ketonuria requiring hospital admission).
- **First-Line Interventions:**
  - Dietary advice: Small, frequent meals (every 2-3 hours) rich in complex carbohydrates (dry crackers, toast, boiled yam/potatoes).
  - Avoid greasy, spicy, fried, or strongly aromatic foods.
  - Hydration: Sip fluids between meals rather than with meals.
  - **Non-pharmacological remedy:** Ginger (1 g to 1.5 g daily of fresh ginger root or ginger tea), chamomile, or pyridoxine (Vitamin B6 10-25 mg three times daily).

### Heartburn and Acid Reflux
- **Clinical Guidance:** Progesterone relaxes the lower esophageal sphincter while the growing uterus elevates intra-abdominal pressure.
- **Management:** Small frequent meals, elevate head of bed during sleep, avoid lying flat within 2 hours of eating. Calcium- or magnesium-based antacids are safe in pregnancy if lifestyle measures fail. Avoid sodium bicarbonate antacids.

### Lower Back and Pelvic Pain
- **Guideline:** Regular physical exercise, maintaining good posture, warm compresses, supportive flat footwear, and maternity support pillows. Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) is the safe first-line analgesic if needed; avoid NSAIDs (Ibuprofen/Diclofenac) especially in the third trimester due to risk of premature closure of ductus arteriosus.

### Constipation
- **Guideline:** Increase intake of dietary fiber (fruits, green vegetables, whole grains), drink at least 2.5 to 3 liters of clean water daily, and engage in gentle physical activity. Wheat bran or psyllium husk can be used safely.

### Leg Cramps and Swelling (Physiological Edema)
- **Guideline:** Gentle calf stretching before bedtime and oral magnesium or calcium supplementation for leg cramps. For mild dependent edema of feet and ankles: elevate legs when resting, avoid tight bands or prolonged standing.
- **DANGER RULE:** Sudden, severe swelling of face, eyes, and hands, particularly when accompanied by headache or vision changes, is NOT physiological edema; it is a cardinal sign of **Pre-Eclampsia** requiring immediate blood pressure assessment and urine protein testing!

## 4. Preventive Measures & Infectious Diseases in Pregnancy
### Malaria Prevention in Pregnancy (IPTp-SP)
- In malaria-endemic zones (including Nigeria), all pregnant women should receive **Intermittent Preventive Treatment in pregnancy with Sulfadoxine-Pyrimethamine (IPTp-SP)** starting in the second trimester (from 13 weeks onwards).
- Administer under Directly Observed Therapy (DOT) at each scheduled ANC visit, with doses given at least one month apart, up to the time of delivery.
- Must sleep inside an Insecticide-Treated Mosquito Net (ITN/LLIN) every night.

### Tetanus Toxoid Immunization
- Administer Tetanus Diphtheria (Td) or Tetanus Toxoid (TT) vaccine according to national schedules (minimum of two doses during pregnancy, 4 weeks apart, with the second dose given at least 2 weeks before delivery) to eliminate neonatal and maternal tetanus.

### Deworming (Anthelminthic Treatment)
- In endemic areas, preventive single-dose oral albendazole (400 mg) or mebendazole (500 mg) is recommended for pregnant women after the first trimester.
